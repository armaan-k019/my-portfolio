# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

About 75% recruiters and hiring managers, 25% research readers.

Recruiters and hiring managers scan quickly. They need company, role, dates, and one piece of proof of work, without clicking through modals to find them.

Research readers (architecture and computation faculty, conference peers, lab collaborators) come for the papers. They need title, venue, status, authors, and a link to the paper.

## Product Purpose

The personal portfolio of Armaan Kazi, a Georgia Tech student double majoring in Computer Science and Architecture. It exists to get him hired and to present his research. Success means a reader can find what he did, where, and when within a minute, and can open the real work (demos, papers, shipped tools) from there.

## Brand Personality

Rigorous, warm, inventive.

- Rigorous: every fact, date and coordinate on the page is real and sourced. Nothing is decorative data.
- Warm: a person, not a template. The existing humor in the copy stays.
- Inventive: the builds (demos, Archipedia, Datum) should feel like they came from someone who makes unexpected things.

## Anti-references

- SaaS landing template: card grids, pill badges, soft shadows, gradient accents, hero metrics.
- Dev-terminal portfolio: dark mode by default, monospace everything, fake shell prompts.
- Boring and generic: a layout that could belong to anyone, where nothing on the page is specific to this person or this work.

## Design Principles

1. Facts in the open. Role, dates and place are visible without a click. Modals hold detail, not basics.
2. Nothing fake. No coordinate, metric or label that is not tied to real data.
3. The work is the imagery. Demos, papers and photographs carry the visual weight, not ambient decoration.
4. His words only. Copy is edited by deletion, never ghostwritten.
5. Commit to the palette. The green and the accent are used with intent, not as 2.5% washes.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA. AA contrast on all text, full keyboard access, prefers-reduced-motion honored, native cursor kept.
