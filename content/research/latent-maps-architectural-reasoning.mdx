## Abstract

Architectural precedent search often collapses design relevance into global visual similarity or coarse metadata, obscuring how and why results are retrieved. We propose a multi-modal precedent retrieval system that emphasizes patch-level visual features, LLM-expanded architectural metadata, and geographic, climatic cues, fused through a user-steerable tri-scalar interface. Instead of relying on geometric analysis or topological metrics, the system reads architectural images, including plans, sections, and perspective, using DINOv2 global and patch embeddings, revealing motif-level correspondences such as repetitive openings, massing edges, structural rhythms, and void patterns. Metadata extracted from ArchDaily is expanded through an LLM to produce consistent spatial-type and contextual descriptors (e.g., "courtyard," "linear bar," "vernacular masonry"), which combine with geolocation-derived climate and density cues. A tri-scalar controller allows designers to adjust the weighting of visual features, metadata, and context in real time, while interactive activation maps provide visual feedback on how the model interprets features within the query. Using a curated set of 9,800+ images from ArchDaily, we demonstrate improved retrieval quality over visual-only baselines, sub-second latency, and enhanced interpretability. The premise is to support part-based architectural reasoning, using patches, not wholes, as the primary unit for guidance. The system foregrounds nuanced, multi-modal precedent relationships, enabling more targeted and explainable design exploration, pairing intensive and extensive knowledge accumulation.
