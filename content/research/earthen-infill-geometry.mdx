Affordable housing in Africa relies on concrete block, and earthen 3D printing offers a lower-carbon, local option. Printable earth is limited by soil mix (Ji et al., 2025) and by overhang and span limits (Sangiorgio et al., 2022). Studies of infill geometry and heat flow focus on printed concrete (Arumugam et al., 2025; Özdemir and Alaçam, 2026), while mashrabiya screens cut afternoon heat gain but did not alone give midday comfort (Bagasi and Calautit, 2020). We found few studies that test infill geometry for printed earth against material, privacy, and airflow goals together. Walls must be buildable, private, and cool at once.

This study will test how open, through-wall infill shapes these three goals in earthen walls for hot-arid and hot-humid African sites. An algorithmic workflow links parametric geometry (cell shape, size, gap, depth), rule-based print checks (span, web, overhang), a privacy score (share of sight lines blocked), and batch computational fluid dynamics (CFD) airflow runs as a cooling proxy, each under set wind and sun inputs. Designs that fail print checks drop out, and the rest are ranked by multi-goal trade-off.

We expect more openness to lift airflow but cut privacy and printability, yet we think cell depth and staggered offsets can split privacy from airflow. Mapping this feasible zone should yield a reusable workflow and clear trade-offs that show which designs merit lab tests before field use. Each climate gets its own ranking.
